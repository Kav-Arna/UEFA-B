## Theory

Dynamic Spaces describes the environment around the ball from the perspective of the players closest to the action. Rather than fixed zones on a pitch, the three "Footballs" are **relative, moving spaces defined by the ball, the ball carrier, and nearby opponents/teammates** — they expand, contract, and shift as play develops. The purpose is to give players a shared vocabulary for what the moment affords them, fitting within an ecological-dynamics / constraints-led view of the game: understanding is built through perceiving and acting within the environment, not memorising fixed patterns.

**Football 1 — Space of Interaction.** The ball possessor and the direct defender. 1v1 or 1v2, a small radius (1–2m) where players have a direct, immediate duel.

**Football 2 — Space of Mutual Help.** The ball possessor, the direct opponent, and the players who could directly support the ball carrier. 2v2, 3v3, and variants — the next layer out.

**Football 3 — Space of Cooperation.** The ball possessor, direct defenders, and everyone else. Players not in the immediate duel still have a job: influencing opponents away from the ball.

## Standard Operating Procedure

**Purpose:** use Football 1/2/3 as a live coaching language during sessions and matches to prompt perception and decision-making, and as an analysis lens afterwards.

**Core coaching questions** (in a stoppage or small-sided game, not as a lecture):
1. *"Are you in Football 1, 2, or 3 right now?"*
2. *"What spaces are there around you?"*
3. *"How can you move to help the player on the ball?"*
4. *"What are your responsibilities in this moment, given you're in Football [1/2/3]?"*

| Football | Who it applies to | Coaching prompt focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ball carrier + direct defender | Can you beat your 1v1/1v2? What's your first touch/body shape doing? |
| 2 | Players in direct combination range | Are you offering an angle? Can you receive, support, or overload? |
| 3 | Everyone else | Are you manipulating the opponent's shape away from the ball? |

**Session design:** small-sided games (2v2–4v4) live almost entirely in Football 1 and 2 — good for isolating duels and combinations. Larger-sided or full-pitch work brings Football 3 into play. Constraints (tight grids vs. wider pitches with floaters) can be used to force a specific Football more often.

**Analysis use:** tag moments by which Football the ball was in; for off-ball players, judge whether their movement matched their Football-2 or Football-3 responsibility. Watch for players physically in Football 3 but *acting* as if they're irrelevant — the tool's key coaching point is that Football 3 still carries responsibility.

## Integration with Other Tools

Dynamic Spaces is a **spatial/role lens** and pairs with the **IJAS Steps** tool, a **directional/progression lens**. Steps answers "where in the pitch, in possession-phase terms, is the ball?"; Football 1/2/3 answers "what is my job relative to the ball right now?"

**Scope at restarts:** Football 1/2/3 applies to any moment that resets into a live, perceptual picture — including a throw-in. It does not apply to fixed structural set-piece patterns such as corners, where players are assigned a pre-set role rather than reading the moment for themselves (see [Principles of Play]({{ '/principles-of-play/' | relative_url }}) §4).

**Player-facing versions:** this page is the coach-side theory. Players receive it via the On-Pitch Prompts card and the illustrated Principles of Play companion.

## Quick Reference

- **Football 1:** ball + direct defender. 1v1/1v2. 1–2m radius.
- **Football 2:** ball + direct opponent + direct supporters. 2v2/3v3 and variants.
- **Football 3:** ball + direct defenders + everyone else. Off-ball manipulation of opponents.
