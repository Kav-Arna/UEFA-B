## 1. In-Possession Principles

### 1.1 Build-Up — Attacking Step 1

- Play out primarily through the two defensive midfielders, who rotate to always present an angle on the half-turn.
- Wide full-backs offer a secondary, wider passing angle to bypass the first line of pressure.
- Goalkeeper and centre-backs split to create an extra number against the opposition's first line.
- If the opposition commits numbers to a high press and central/wide outlets are covered, bypass the press directly to the attacking midfield three or the striker, who lay off or flick into the space vacated behind the press.

Step 2's rule (three central lanes for the attacking three, a fourth on the ball side, never both wide lanes) governs the attacking three once the ball is past the first line. Step 1 build-up is a different mechanism — escaping the press, not combining once beyond it — and deliberately occupies all five lanes to do it: both full-backs push high and wide simultaneously (the one deliberate exception to "never both wide lanes"), the ball-far pivot drops centrally between the centre-backs, and the ball-near pivot reads the opposition's screen to show in the half-space or centrally.

### 1.2 Progression — Attacking Step 2 (Narrow Structure and Width Triggers)

The team occupies three of five vertical lanes through the attacking midfield three, who stay connected to each other and to the double pivot — the platform for combination play and quick vertical passing.

**Width triggers**

- Width is not held permanently — it is provided on the ball side, either by the full-back or by a defensive midfielder stepping into an auxiliary attacking-midfield role.
- If the ball-side wide attacking midfielder holds the touchline, the full-back underlaps into the inside channel to combine centrally.
- If the ball-side wide attacking midfielder tucks inside, the full-back overlaps outside to provide width — occupying a fourth lane on the ball side. The far-side full-back and attacking midfielder stay inside; the team never occupies more than four lanes at once.
- The trigger is the first sign of the ball beginning to shift toward a side, not its arrival there.

**Creating and exploiting 2v1s**

- A player in possession dribbles directly at a defender to pin or bait — fixing his attention and drawing a covering player across. If he jumps to press, the covering pass is on. If he holds his position, the space he concedes is the ball carrier's to drive into.
- Support players move to create a passing lane off that fixed defender — an underlap, overlap, or third-man run — rather than standing in flat lines.
- Players away from the immediate combination manipulate the opponents who aren't part of the 2v1, occupying or dragging markers to stop them recovering across.

### 1.3 Creation and Finishing — Attacking Steps 3–4

The striker pins the last centre-back or spins in behind on the blind side. The two wide attacking midfielders and the 10 support underneath or sprint beyond as the second and third attackers. Crosses are delivered from the half-space — cut-backs or low driven balls, timed for runs to the near post, back post, and edge of the box.

## 2. Out-of-Possession Principles

### 2.1 Defensive Shape

Stay narrow and compact, defending primarily across three central lanes and inviting the opponent into the wide areas. Maintain a maximum of roughly 10 metres between the defensive line and the front line of pressure. Units shuffle across together, staying connected lane to lane.

### 2.2 Defending Process — Delay, Deny, Direct, Dispossess

| Principle | Coaching Detail |
|---|---|
| Delay | First defender engages to control space and time — jockeys and shows the opponent away from goal rather than diving into a tackle. |
| Deny | Cover players deny central passing lanes and access to the opposition's key central players, forcing the ball away from the middle. |
| Direct | Pressing and screening angles channel the opponent into wide areas, away from goal-threatening central zones. |
| Dispossess | Once directed wide or into a pressing trigger, the team presses in numbers to win the ball. If stretched, concede territory not central access, and clear first-time. |

This sequence repeats at whichever Defending Step the ball currently sits in — Step 1 when the front line engages, Step 2 midfield vs. midfield, Step 3 back line vs. front line.

## 3. Transition Principles

### 3.1 Attack to Defence (Counter-Press)

The nearest player to the ball presses immediately to delay the counter-attack. The next two closest players cover and support, cutting off the most dangerous passing options. Everyone else recovers goal-side and narrow, cutting passing lanes rather than chasing the ball. The objective is to regain possession within the first few seconds, or force the opponent backwards long enough to re-establish shape.

### 3.2 Defence to Attack

On winning the ball, scan immediately for a forward option — in behind, into the attacking three, or to the striker — before considering a safe recycling pass. Look to exploit disorganisation with a quick, direct pass into a half-space. Where no forward option exists, retain possession through the double pivot or full-backs and re-establish the in-possession principles.

## 4. Set Pieces

This section covers fixed structural dead-ball routines — corners, and by extension any restart delivered as a pre-assigned structural pattern rather than a perceptual read. These sit outside the Step/Space framework used elsewhere in this document, because a corner routine is a fixed pattern assigned in advance, not a phase the ball moves through or a space a player perceives and self-organises within.

**Not every restart is a set piece in this sense.** A throw-in is a dead ball, but it isn't a fixed structural pattern — it resets play into an ordinary Step 1 picture with a live Space 1/2/3 read, the same as any other moment the ball changes hands near the touchline. The dividing line is whether the moment is a pre-assigned pattern (a corner, or a rehearsed free-kick routine close to goal) or a live perceptual restart (a throw-in, or a free kick taken quickly into open play).

### 4.1 Corners — Attacking Structure

| Role | Job |
|---|---|
| Taker | Delivery |
| Front four, in a line | 1 — Back post run · 2 — Penalty spot run · 3 — Striker: target zone · 4 — Keeper run |
| 2 players, edge of box | Ready to react to a second ball — first line of the counter-press trigger |
| CDM | Holds the middle, midway between halfway line and the D |
| 2 defenders | Positioned to react to a clearance |
| GK | Edge of the D — sweeping range |

**One thing:** *Know your run, hit your zone.*

### 4.2 Corners — Defensive Structure

| Role | Job |
|---|---|
| #9 | Central, midway between halfway line and the D — reacts to clearances |
| #10 | Edge of the box — knockdowns and poorly struck clearances |
| Ball-side attacking midfielder | Covers the short corner or a poorly hit delivery |
| Everyone else | Man-for-man: goal-side and ball-side of their attacker |
| Spare players | Double up on the opposition's biggest attacking threat |

**One thing:** *Know your role, pick your mark, be strong.*

## 5. Coaching Application — UEFA B Considerations

Delivered through a progressive block of practices moving from isolated technical/positional detail to full opposed phase-of-play and transition games — a whole-part-whole approach within a constraints-led methodology.

**Suggested practice types:** positional rondos with directional bias; small-sided constraint games rewarding half-space combinations; position-specific full-back practices isolating the underlap/overlap decision; phase-of-play practices against a high press; pressing-trigger games conditioning the counter-press roles; functional finishing from the half-space.

## 6. Player-Facing Translation

The detail above is the coaching and analysis layer. Players receive a plain-language translation in two forms: the On-Pitch Prompts / U17 Player Card (compressed, pitchside) and a fully illustrated version of this section (fuller, with a worked diagram for each moment). The translation follows one rule: **Space (Football 1/2/3) is the only technical vocabulary spoken to players in the moment** — see [Dynamic Spaces]({{ '/dynamic-spaces/' | relative_url }}).

| Player-facing headline | Translates | Space 1 cue | Space 2 cue | Space 3 cue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Which Space am I in? | The standing self-organisation question | Pin or bait — he jumps, pass. He holds, drive into the space. | Show for the pass. | Drag your marker, open the lane. |
| Beat the next line | Attacking Steps 1–4; three-lane base, ball-side fourth lane | — | — | — |
| Squeeze the middle, show them wide | Delay–Deny–Direct–Dispossess; narrow block | Show, don't dive in. | Block the pass to their key players. | Stay narrow and connected. |
| First to react wins it back | Counter-press roles | Press now. | Block the danger. | Goal-side, narrow, don't chase. |
