## 1. Identity Statement

We are a team built around a narrow, connected 1-4-2-3-1: we progress the ball vertically and quickly through a compact central structure, provide width only on the ball side as a deliberate trigger rather than a fixed occupation, and look to create 2v1 situations through players dribbling to pin or bait opponents, supported by movement that opens a passing lane. Without the ball we defend narrow and compact across three central lanes, delaying, denying central access, directing the opponent wide, and dispossessing when the moment is right. This identity is built to be coachable within a grassroots development environment, and it exists to serve the wider aim set out in the [Coaching Philosophy]({{ '/coaching-philosophy/' | relative_url }}): development first, and a lasting love of the game.

## 2. Context and Constraints

- **Level:** youth grassroots / development football — the model is built for this level, not adapted down from an elite one.
- **Training frequency:** two sessions per week in August, September and the first half of October; one session per week from mid-October onward. Session efficiency becomes critical from that point.
- The pre-match warm-up is used as a further coaching opportunity: a single representative activity that rehearses the majority of what is needed in that week's match, worth roughly another 15 minutes of coaching.
- Because contact time is limited, the model is deliberately built on a small number of high-leverage principles rather than many complex patterns — consistent with a constraints-led approach that changes behaviour through a few well-chosen rules, not a long list of instructions.

## 3. Formation: 1-4-2-3-1

The base structure is a back four, a double pivot, three attacking midfielders in the half-spaces and central lane, and a single striker.

| Position | One-Line Identity |
|---|---|
| GK | Extra passing option; splits or drops to overload a high press. |
| 4 (Back Four) | Split to progress; hold the central lane out of possession. |
| 2 (Double Pivot) | Primary build-up outlet; one steps up as auxiliary width when needed. |
| 3 (Attacking Midfield) | Narrow — occupy the half-spaces and central lane; connect and combine. |
| 1 (Striker) | Pins the last defender or spins in behind; leads the press out of possession. |

## 4. The Four Moments — Headline Summary

Full coaching detail for each of these moments — including the underlap/overlap trigger, the Delay-Deny-Direct-Dispossess sequence, and the counter-press roles — is set out in [Principles of Play]({{ '/principles-of-play/' | relative_url }}). This section gives the headline identity only.

**In Possession** — Play out through the double pivot or wide full-backs; go direct to the attacking three or striker if the opposition presses high with numbers. Progress through a narrow attacking three, with width provided on the ball side by the full-back or a stepping pivot player. Bias to vertical, quick passing and 2v1 creation. In the final third, the striker pins or spins in behind, the attacking three supply or sprint beyond, and crosses come from the half-space.

**Out of Possession** — Narrow and compact across three central lanes, roughly 10 metres between the lines. Delay first, deny central access, direct the opponent wide, then dispossess — or defend the emergency if stretched.

**Transition to Defence** — Nearest player presses immediately; the next two cover; everyone else recovers narrow and goal-side, cutting passing lanes rather than chasing the ball.

**Transition to Attack** — Look forward first — in behind, into the attacking three, or to the striker — before a safe recycling pass, aiming to exploit the opponent's disorganisation before their shape resets.

## 5. Non-Negotiables

- Open, sideways-on body shape when receiving, to play forward or away from pressure.
- Three central lanes always occupied by the attacking three; a fourth lane opens on the ball side only when a width trigger fires — never both wide lanes at once.
- Maximum of roughly 10 metres between the defensive and front lines out of possession.
- Nearest player presses immediately on losing the ball; no exceptions for position or fatigue.
- Selection serves development first; at this age group rotation is now narrow, focused on strengthening players in their main position rather than wide rotation, in line with the Coaching Philosophy.

## 6. Season Development Arc

**August to mid-October** (two sessions per week): embed the detail. The technical picture the model depends on — body shape, lane occupation, pressing triggers — is coached explicitly and reinforced through representative small-sided practices.

**Mid-October onward** (one session per week): maintain and sharpen rather than introduce. High-leverage constraint games and the pre-match warm-up activity carry the model, with the week's single coaching focus chosen from whichever one or two principles the last match showed were most needed.
