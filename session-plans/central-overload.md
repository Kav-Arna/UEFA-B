## Theme

Where the three-session ladder (First Touch → Drag the Cover → Free the Second Man) spreads the thin/thick/full ramp across separate sessions, this is the same ramp compressed into a single 60-minute session — built specifically for UEFA B delivery and assessment.

## Structure

| Phase | Duration | Slice | Picture |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm-up / technical | 10 min | — | Passing and receiving on the half-turn, no opposition |
| Central duel | 12 min | Thin | 1v1+1 in the double-pivot zone — a CDM in possession against a direct opponent, reading jump-or-hold |
| Overload building | 18 min | Thick | 3v2 — the attacking three combine against two defenders, scoring conditioned on the pin-or-bait read starting the move |
| Phase of play | 20 min | Full | 7v6+GK — build-up through the pivot into the attacking three and striker, against a mid-block, normal scoring plus the double-goal 2v1 bonus |

## Coaching Focus by Phase

**Thin (Central duel):** the CDM's first job — beat the press or draw it, per [Principles of Play §1.2]({{ '/principles-of-play/' | relative_url }}). No support players yet; the point is an unambiguous read.

**Thick (Overload building):** the attacking three's connection to each other and to the double pivot — three lanes as the base, a fourth only on the ball side. This is where the width-trigger logic (underlap if the wide player holds, overlap if he tucks) gets its first live rep.

**Full (Phase of play):** everything at once — build-up, progression, and the striker pinning or spinning against a recovering back line. This phase is the assessable centrepiece: an observer should be able to see Step 1 through Step 4 and the Space 1/2/3 reads happening inside a genuine 7v6+GK picture.

## UEFA B Notes

This session is designed to give an assessor a single, observable thread from theory to practice: the same pin-or-bait mechanic named in Principles of Play, visible first in isolation (thin), then in combination (thick), then inside a representative phase of play (full) — without re-explaining the theme at each transition.
